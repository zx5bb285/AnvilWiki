## The Build That Fights After You Stop

Most Hero Siege builds answer one question: how hard did the hit land? The Demonspawn answers a different one — **how long does the hit keep landing?** Its community-documented tools, **Bone Barrage** and **Blood Tendrils**, belong to the damage-over-time family: what you cast keeps working after your attention has moved on, and the fight you started is not the fight that ends.

That single difference changes the whole feel of the class, which is why this page is organized around the playstyle rather than around a skill list.

## Two Tools, Two Philosophies

Community sources name two tools, and they read as two ways of applying the same idea:

- **Bone Barrage** is the frontal application — damage you put onto a target and let run.
- **Blood Tendrils** reads as the spreading one — the class's answer to the fact that damage-over-time is only as good as how many enemies it reaches.

The pairing is the class's actual thesis: apply your damage broadly, let it work while you move, and treat every fight as something you set in motion rather than something you finish with one press. It is the same family of thinking the [Plague Doctor's](/guides/hero-siege-plague-doctor-build/) affliction playstyle lives in, and the two classes are natural discussions to have next to each other.

## Why Damage-Over-Time Changes How You Play

Adopting a persistent-damage identity rewires three habits:

1. **Opening replaces finishing.** You lead with application, then reposition and let the fight resolve. A player who keeps swinging at a target that is already ticking is wasting the class's whole advantage.
2. **Coverage beats focus.** Because the damage spreads, the right question is "did I reach the pack?" rather than "did I burst the elite?" — a mental shift that pays off across the campaign's trash-heavy stretches.
3. **Patience is a stat.** Damage-over-time rewards players who can trust a plan and wait for it. That is a real skill, and the [beginner guide](/guides/hero-siege-beginner-guide/) treats hesitation as a mistake for burst classes and a virtue here.

## The Curve: Slow Start, Loud Middle, Honest End

The Demonspawn's progression does not feel like everyone else's, and pretending otherwise would mislead a new player:

- **Early levels are the weakest stretch.** With a small toolkit, delayed damage simply kills slower than a direct hit. This is the phase where most players quit a damage-over-time class — knowing it is coming is half the answer.
- **Mid-game is where it clicks.** Once the kit is complete, the class clears in a rhythm no burst class matches: apply, move, watch. This is the stretch where its identity finally pays.
- **The endgame is the standard question.** Like every class, what a build becomes in deep content depends on gear and [tree](/guides/hero-siege-ether-tree-guide/) commitment — and the [tier list](/guides/hero-siege-class-tier-list/) is where community judgment on that lives, not here.

## Free Since Season 9 — What That Changed

Community season coverage places the Demonspawn among the classes that went free in Season 9, kept available through cosmetic skins rather than a purchase. Practically, that means the class's cost of entry is a character slot — which makes it a cheap way to test whether persistent damage suits you before you make it [your season's main](/guides/hero-siege-league-starter-builds/).

A free class is also the cheapest form of a good habit: try the playstyle, keep it if it fits, and do not force it if the slow opening grates. The Demonspawn's whole design asks for patience, and a season is long enough to know whether you have it.

## Demonspawn FAQ

### Does the Demonspawn feel like a summoner?

It shares the "damage working independently of you" feeling, which is why players describe it that way — but it does not depend on pets the way a true summoner does. Community documentation describes persistent damage tools, not a companion army.

### How does it handle boss fights?

Bosses reward sustained damage well, since a boss is exactly where persistent effects do not go to waste. The classic caution applies: [Uber](/bosses/hero-siege-uber-bosses-guide/) encounters punish standing still, so application-then-move is the habit that carries over from clearing.

### Is it a good alt for a player who already has a main?

It is one of the better ones — free to acquire since Season 9, easy to fund from a main's [economy](/guides/hero-siege-gold-farming-guide/), and different enough from a burst class to feel like a new game rather than a reskin of the same one.
