## What Do the First Three Acts Cover?

Acts 1 through 3 are the last stretch of Hero Siege that lets you win on autopilot — and the desert at the end of Act 3 is where the autopilot switches off. Everything before that point is the game teaching: how your character moves, how your skills chain together, and how the campaign expects you to keep improving while the map keeps pushing you forward.

The walking route through the teaching half is short and linear:

| Act | Zone | Landmarks, in walking order | Act boss |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Forests of Tarethiel | Outskirts of Inoya → Fields of Battle → Pumpkin Patch → The Village → King's Garden | Gurag |
| 2 | The Glacial Tundra | Crystal Village → Chilling Lake → Arctic Tundra → Snowy Mountains → Frozen Lands | Grim Reaper |
| 3 | Deserts of Mos'Arathim | Mos'Arathim Village → Dry Hills → Mos'Arathim Desert → Pyramid L1 → Pyramid L2 | Anubis |

Each act plays as a corridor: clear the zone, activate the waypoint, kill the boss, take the door. The full nine-act ladder — and how the same fights repeat on Nightmare, Hell and Inferno — is mapped in the [act bosses guide](/bosses/hero-siege-act-bosses-guide/). This page stays with the three acts a brand-new character meets first.

## What Does Act 1 Set Up?

Act 1 is deliberately generous. The walk from the Outskirts of Inoya through King's Garden hands you gear, gold and levels fast enough that mistakes cost almost nothing, which is exactly what makes it the right place to build habits instead of just clearing maps. The [beginner guide's classic mistakes](/guides/hero-siege-beginner-guide/) all start here: lingering in side zones for perfect drops, hoarding low-tier uniques, postponing the campaign push.

Fields of Battle, the act's second landmark, is worth naming for a second reason: community leveling routes treat it as one of the classic reset-and-rekill grinding spots in the game. Even if you never farm it on purpose, the takeaway is the tempo this act rewards — fast kills in a compact map beat slow clears anywhere.

The act's boss, [Gurag](/bosses/hero-siege-gurag-boss/), is the first fight with a real single-target requirement. He is also the source of Gurag's Soul, the opening piece of a six-part recipe that runs through the first six acts. You do not need to care about that recipe yet — the campaign collects it for you as long as you keep moving.

## Why Does Act 2 Test Your Sustain?

The Glacial Tundra is longer and meaner than anything in Tarethiel, and its lesson is attrition. Enemy pressure climbs across the chain from Crystal Village out to the Frozen Lands, and a character who has been ignoring sustain — recovery, movement, resource management — feels it long before any boss does. Act 2 is where "keep moving" stops being a slogan and becomes the difference between a clean map and a slow, expensive one.

Its boss, the [Grim Reaper](/bosses/hero-siege-reaper-boss/), adds Death's Sigil to the recipe line. If you find yourself genuinely stalled in the snow rather than just moving slowly, the fix is nearly always movement and sustain — not a farming detour. Nothing you grind here survives the difficulty ladder anyway.

## Why Does Act 3 Stop Rewarding Gear Stacking?

Act 3 is the teaching half's final exam, and the [Anubis fight](/bosses/hero-siege-anubis-boss/) is the reason so many first runs stall in Mos'Arathim. Through Acts 1 and 2, upgrades explain themselves: a better weapon clears faster, newer armor dies less. The desert breaks that relationship. Anubis does not ask what your gear score is; he asks whether your talents and skills add up to a plan — in practice, a defined single-target answer that survives a full fight without running dry.

That is the difference between a gear check and a consistency check. A gear check is solved by farming; a consistency check is solved by deciding what your character actually is. If the desert fight is outlasting you, read your talent allocation before you read the market.

<Callout type="warn" title="Act 3 stalls are build problems first">
The most common Act 3 mistake is treating the wall as a farming problem. Farming closes gear gaps; it does not fix a build with no dedicated single-target skill. The [starter builds page](/guides/hero-siege-league-starter-builds/) exists for exactly this correction.
</Callout>

The act also ends with a dungeon tail — Pyramid L1 into Pyramid L2 — and the desert rewards exploration along the way. Community leveling routes regularly fold side content such as Unstable Rifts and Chaos Towers into this stretch for the experience, because fast kills anywhere translate into levels; the [leveling guide](/guides/hero-siege-leveling-guide/) covers that tempo logic in full. Anubis caps the act and adds Anubis' Ankh to the recipe.

## When Is It Time to Move Past Act 3?

The signal is kill speed, not completion. If Anubis fell to a build that still felt coherent — packs dying fast, resources holding — Act 4 is ready for you. If the win came from respawns and consumables, spend one zone fixing the build first, because the wall half of the campaign asks the same question with less patience. The walkthrough continues in [Acts 4-6](/guides/hero-siege-act-walkthrough-4-6/), where Damien's mining region opens that conversation.

## Acts 1-3 FAQ

**Is Act 2 skippable?**
No. The campaign is linear — every act boss must fall before the next act opens, on every difficulty.

**What are the Pyramid floors in Act 3?**
Pyramid L1 and L2 are the dungeon tail of the Mos'Arathim chain, and community leveling routes have long treated them as one of the classic grinding spots on the way through — kill-speed experience in a compact space. Exact reset behavior is patch-sensitive, so verify it in-game rather than trusting a specific number.

**Which class should I take through Acts 1-3?**
Whichever one you will actually learn. The teaching half is forgiving enough that every class on the [tier list](/guides/hero-siege-class-tier-list/) clears it comfortably, and the build-coherence lesson waiting at Act 3 is the same for all of them.
