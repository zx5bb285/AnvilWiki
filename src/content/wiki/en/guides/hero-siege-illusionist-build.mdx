## The Class Nobody Names First

Ask a Hero Siege player for their favorite class and you will hear the loud ones — the [meme that climbed](/guides/hero-siege-redneck-build/), the [new class still being figured out](/guides/hero-siege-prophet-build/), the specialist that does one thing spectacularly. You will not often hear **Illusionist**. It does not have a hook, and community tier discussion accordingly files it in the [A-tier](/guides/hero-siege-class-tier-list/) as an **all-rounder** — a verdict that sounds like faint praise and is actually the most practical sentence on the list.

This page is the argument for reading "all-rounder" as a recommendation.

## What "A-Tier All-Rounder" Buys You

A season is not one fight. It is a campaign, a [difficulty climb](/guides/hero-siege-difficulty-guide/), a farming routine, and an [Uber](/bosses/hero-siege-uber-bosses-guide/) progression — and every one of those stages stresses a different part of a build. A specialist is designed to be excellent in one of those stages and merely survives the rest. An all-rounder is designed to be good in all of them.

That design has concrete value:

- **No dead stage.** There is no phase of the season where the class's one trick goes silent, which is exactly when a specialist player stalls out.
- **No forced detour.** Specialists often build a second gear set or a second character to cover their gap; the all-rounder's gap is small enough to live with.
- **Predictable investment.** Gear and [Ether Tree](/guides/hero-siege-ether-tree-guide/) nodes that improve the class improve all of its jobs, because the class's jobs are not wildly different.

<Callout type="info" title="A-tier as a plateau, not a peak">
"A-tier all-rounder" is community shorthand for a class with a high floor and a modest ceiling. That is a description of where it sits in Season 10 discussion, not a promise about any single patch — tier lists move, and the class's steady reputation is what makes it a safe read in the meantime.
</Callout>

## Why Correct Beats Exciting in a Fresh Season

Season 10 is weeks old, and the whole roster's documentation is still settling — the [new class's notes are contradicted by a patch](/guides/hero-siege-prophet-build/), the [tier list](/guides/hero-siege-class-tier-list/) is community-held rather than datamined, and the meta is a moving target. That environment rewards exactly the choice an all-rounder is: a class with no single assumption to get wrong.

Pick a specialist on a bad read of the patch and a nerf can hollow out your season. Pick the all-rounder and the worst case is that you were never the strongest — which, in a season where being correct matters more than being optimal, is a fine place to stand.

## Who Should Actually Pick It

The honest audience for this page is narrow and specific:

- **Players returning after a long break.** The all-rounder does not require you to know the current meta to function; it functions while you relearn it.
- **Players who will only make one character.** If your season is one class, pick the one with no missing stage.
- **Players whose goal is completion, not ranking.** Clearing the [campaign](/bosses/hero-siege-act-bosses-guide/) and reaching the [endgame](/guides/hero-siege-wormhole-guide/) systems is the class's whole brief, and it does that brief well.

It is the wrong pick if you play to feel the ceiling of a build. The [tier list's](/guides/hero-siege-class-tier-list/) S-tier entries exist for that, and choosing the Illusionist while wanting their highlight is how a player ends up bored by a class that did nothing wrong.

## The Cost of Being Fine at Everything

The price is stated plainly: **you will rarely be the reason a group wins.** An all-rounder does not carry a party through a fight that needs a specialist's peak — it holds the line while someone else provides it. If you are comfortable being the reliable half of a team rather than the memorable one, this class's trade is a straight upgrade. If you are not, no amount of tier-list argument will make it feel like one.

## Illusionist FAQ

### Is the Illusionist's A-tier rating a real endorsement?

It is an endorsement of its reliability, which is the thing A-tier all-rounders are rewarded for. Read it as "difficult to regret," not "best in class" — those are different claims, and the [tier list](/guides/hero-siege-class-tier-list/) keeps both kinds of entry side by side.

### How does it compare to the Nomad, the other all-rounder?

Both classes earn their standing by covering ground rather than by specializing, which is why community discussion groups them. The distinction is in how each one is built rather than in the label — read the [Nomad build](/guides/hero-siege-nomad-build/) and judge by the playstyle, not the tag.

### Should a brand-new player start with it?

It is one of the safer first picks, for the same reason it is a safe returning pick: nothing about it depends on knowledge you do not have yet. The [beginner guide](/guides/hero-siege-beginner-guide/) still applies — an all-rounder forgives a build mistake without forgiving a fundamental one.
