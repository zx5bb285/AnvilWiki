## The Class That Was a Punchline

Every roster has one, and for a long stretch the Redneck was Hero Siege's: a class the community treated as a bit rather than a choice. That reputation was never really about the numbers — it was about the fantasy. A class introduced as a joke gets read as a joke, and once a community agrees on that, its players have to earn their respect one boss at a time.

Season 10 is where that finally paid off. Community tier discussion now places the **Chainsaw Slash** Redneck in the [S-tier](/guides/hero-siege-class-tier-list/) endgame tier. The punchline became a build guide.

## What Changed

The honest answer is "one build," and this page will not pretend otherwise. Chainsaw Slash is what community sources document as the Redneck's S-tier route — a melee-flavored build that performs in the deep end rather than only in the campaign. The existence of a single S-tier build does not redeem an entire class's history; it does mean that the class's ceiling is real.

<Callout type="info" title="Community tier, not a spreadsheet">
S-tier is a community-held judgment about Season 10, not a datamined damage table. It reflects where experienced players think the build currently sits. That is enough to take it seriously and not enough to promise it survives the next patch.
</Callout>

## Why the Reversal Is Worth Understanding

The Redneck's climb says something useful about how to read a tier list during a fresh season. Reputation and power are different axes, and they drift apart in both directions:

- **A meme class can be strong.** Tier lists measure output, not dignity. If the [top group](/guides/hero-siege-class-tier-list/) says Chainsaw Slash belongs, the class's old reputation is noise.
- **A popular class can be ordinary.** The reverse happens just as often, and the same list settles it.
- **One strong build is not a class identity.** The Redneck's S-tier status is a claim about Chainsaw Slash specifically — a player who ignores that build and plays the class "for the vibe" is not playing the tier list's Redneck.

## Playing It Means Committing to the Bit

There is a real cost to picking a class the community still jokes about, and it is mostly social until it becomes mechanical. If you build the Redneck half-seriously — split across whatever looks fun, ignoring the one route that earned the tier — you get the meme's outcomes rather than the meta's. The class rewards committing to Chainsaw Slash the way every deep-end build rewards commitment, and it punishes hedging the way a joke class punishes being taken lightly.

The good news is that the path is the standard one. Campaign and ladder with the [beginner guide's](/guides/hero-siege-beginner-guide/) discipline, feed the [Ether](/guides/hero-siege-ether-tree-guide/) and [Incarnation](/guides/hero-siege-incarnation-tree-guide/) trees toward the build's damage plan, and let the [farming loop](/guides/hero-siege-gold-farming-guide/) fund the [gear](/items/hero-siege-uniques-overview/) that turns a memed class into a real one.

## The Honest Counterargument

None of this makes the Redneck a safe recommendation. A class carried by one build is a class with one point of failure: if a patch touches Chainsaw Slash, the tier argument moves with it, and the players least prepared for that are the ones who picked it because "meme class is secretly S-tier" was a fun sentence.

If you like the archetype regardless of tier, the Redneck is a good pick and Chainsaw Slash is a strong tool. If you are chasing the tier and nothing else, know that you are betting on one build in a season that will keep patching — which is a bet, not a strategy, and the [tier list](/guides/hero-siege-class-tier-list/) will tell you soon enough whether it held.

## Redneck FAQ

### Was the Redneck always a joke class?

Community reputation long treated it that way, but reputation lags performance. The accurate version is that its reputation was a joke while its standing was simply low — and Chainsaw Slash is what changed the second part.

### Does Chainsaw Slash work for leveling too?

Community documentation frames it as the class's S-tier *endgame* build, which is the tier that matters for this page's claim. Its leveling performance is a separate question, and the [league starter page](/guides/hero-siege-league-starter-builds/) is the place to compare starting routes across the roster.

### How similar is it to other melee builds?

The two-handed pressure classes — [Marauder](/guides/hero-siege-marauder-build/) and [Butcher](/guides/hero-siege-butcher-build/) — share the "close the distance and swing" instinct, but Chainsaw Slash is its own build with its own damage pattern. Sharing a range does not make two builds interchangeable.
