## Act 9's Ending, and Season 10's Flagship Fight

Cthulhu is the last boss in Hero Siege's campaign and the headline encounter of [Season 10 (Ebontharn)](/guides/hero-siege-season-10-guide/). He sits at the end of Act 9, inside the **Abyssal Realm**, and he is not an optional side encounter — he is where the story stops and where the difficulty ladder unlocks.

That double role makes him the most consequential fight in the game right now. Kill him and the campaign is finished; kill him at the right difficulty and the next tier of endgame opens. Every other Uber you read about assumes you have been through the campaign, and Cthulhu is the door.

## The Odin Prerequisite and the Nightmare-to-Hell Gate

Access is gated in two layers, and both matter.

The first gate is **Odin, Act 8's boss**. Until Odin goes down, Act 9 does not open at all — there is no shortcut, no crafted key, no artifact route. Everything the community documents about reaching Cthulhu starts from "you have beaten Odin." Our [Act bosses guide](/bosses/hero-siege-act-bosses-guide/) covers the full act-by-act structure and where Odin fits.

The second gate is difficulty. Clearing Act 9 and Cthulhu on **Normal** unlocks **Nightmare**. Clearing him again on Nightmare is what unlocks **Hell**. This is not a formality — it is a deliberate structure that makes the endgame ladder start with repeat Cthulhu kills. The [difficulty guide](/guides/hero-siege-difficulty-guide/) explains how that escalation reshapes the rest of your progression.

<Callout type="warn" title="Cthulhu is a progression gate, not a farm target">
Because every difficulty tier runs through him once, treat your first kills as unlocking, not farming. The loot conversation starts after Hell is open — until then, the point is the gate, not the drop table.
</Callout>

## What the Abyssal Realm Throws at You

The community's documentation of the fight records five abilities. This page describes them by behavior, not by damage, because tuning moves with difficulty and patch — read any specific number as something to verify in-game.

- **Ether Constructs.** Cthulhu raises constructs from raw ether. They exist to split your attention and fill the arena, which makes the fight's movement puzzle harder while he cycles his own attacks. Clear them or they compound.
- **Void Tether.** A tether that links you to the boss or to a point in the arena. The community's framing is that it constrains where you can safely move — the counter is to break the link or reposition before it resolves, not to brace and take it.
- **Ethereal Destruction.** An area attack built to punish standing in the wrong place. Like every large telegraph in the game, the damage is avoidable; the danger is being trapped in it by a tether or a construct.
- **Legion of Ether.** A wider summoning that floods the arena with ether-born adds. This is the ability that turns a clean arena into a crowded one, and it is the reason area-of-effect clear is worth carrying into the fight.
- **Vorpal Strike.** The fight's direct, punishing hit — the one you never want to eat. Treat it as the reason to respect every other mechanic: getting clipped by a construct and then caught by Vorpal Strike is the standard death pattern.

Notice the shape of it: Cthulhu's kit is built to *remove your options*. Constructs and Legions crowd the floor, Void Tether constrains your movement, and Ethereal Destruction and Vorpal Strike punish whatever space you have left. The fight is won by keeping the arena clean and never letting two mechanics overlap.

## The 30-Second Opening Cutscene (Read This First)

The single most practical piece of Cthulhu advice in the community, and the one that saves the most wasted attempts: **the fight opens with roughly a 30-second forced cutscene before combat begins.**

<Callout type="tip" title="Wait for the cutscene to end before you buff">
Do not pre-emptively pop buffs, long cooldowns or consumables while the cutscene is playing. They tick down during the scripted sequence and are gone before the first real mechanic lands. The community's recommendation is uniform: let the cutscene finish, then start your setup. Players who ignore this enter the fight with their entire opening window already spent.
</Callout>

It is a small thing that costs almost nothing to get right and a lot to get wrong, which is exactly the kind of detail this tier rewards.

## How Cthulhu Fits the Rest of the Endgame

Because Act 9 is the campaign's terminus, Cthulhu sits above the pair of "Uber tiers" in the player's mind without being an Uber himself. The [Uber bosses overview](/bosses/hero-siege-uber-bosses-guide/) maps where the superboss encounters sit; the [classic trio](/bosses/hero-siege-classic-uber-bosses/) and the Season 10 newcomers like [Phantom Leviathan](/bosses/hero-siege-phantom-leviathan/) are the endgame content that follows once Hell is unlocked and the campaign is behind you.

So the honest placement is this: Cthulhu is the last campaign fight and the gate to the endgame, not its hardest exam. Clear him to open Hell, farm the Uber rotation to build power, and come back to him at higher difficulty for the unlocks that follow.

## Preparing for the Campaign's Final Fight

1. **Finish Odin first.** There is no path around the prerequisite. If Act 9 is not open, the answer is Act 8, not a build tweak.
2. **Build for a crowded arena.** Ether Constructs and Legion of Ether mean the floor fills up. A build with area clear handles Cthulhu's pressure far better than a pure single-target setup.
3. **Respect the movement mechanics.** Void Tether and Ethereal Destruction are about where you are allowed to stand. Mobility that keeps a lane open is worth more here than raw damage.
4. **Do not rush the opening.** Wait out the cutscene, then buff. It is the cheapest edge you will ever get on a final boss.
5. **Accept that Hell will take a second kill.** Plan for at least two Cthulhu clears — one to finish the campaign, one on the next difficulty to unlock the tier above it.

## Cthulhu FAQ

### Is Cthulhu the hardest boss in Hero Siege?

No — that distinction belongs to the endgame Uber tier, which unlocks and scales above the campaign. Cthulhu is the campaign's hardest fight and the gate to the endgame, which is a different kind of title. Being good enough to beat him is the entry requirement for the fights that are harder.

### How long is the opening cutscene before the Cthulhu fight?

Community reporting puts it at roughly 30 seconds. The exact length is not important; the habit is. Wait for it to finish before you spend any buffs or cooldowns, because anything you use during the sequence expires before the fight truly starts.

### Do I have to kill Cthulhu more than once?

To advance difficulty, yes. Clearing Act 9 and Cthulhu on Normal unlocks Nightmare, and you must beat him again on Nightmare to unlock Hell. The campaign's final boss is also its most-required repeat kill.

### Why do my attempts fall apart late in the fight?

The most common pattern is a construct or Legion add clipping you while you are committed to dodging a big telegraphed attack — the fight is designed so that mess gives the boss a free hit. Keep the arena clean between abilities and the late-fight chaos stops happening.
