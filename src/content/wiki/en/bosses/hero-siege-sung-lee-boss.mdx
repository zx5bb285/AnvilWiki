## Why Sung Lee Is a Positioning Exam

The [classic trio](/bosses/hero-siege-classic-uber-bosses/) each isolate one Uber-tier skill, and Sung Lee's is distance. Virtually every ability in the fight is either a melee-range punish or a ground hazard that punishes standing still. There is no phase where the correct answer is "hold your ground and burn the boss" — the fight is a continuous question about where you are standing and when you are allowed to stop.

That is why the community calls him the trio's exam. Damage alone does not clear him. A player who has learned Reaper's endurance and [Damien's](/bosses/hero-siege-damien-boss/) burst windows still has to prove they can read a shrinking arena.

## Paying the Toll: 50 Battle Fragments

Sung Lee is not behind a crafted key or an artifact set the way some Ubers are. Entry is a toll: **50 Battle Fragments**, paid to a portal into the **Eternal Battlefields** that appears at random on Hell-difficulty maps.

<Callout type="tip" title="Keep a surplus, farm the portal when it appears">
Because the portal is a random occurrence rather than a fixed waypoint, the practical habit is to bank Battle Fragments well past 50 so a spawn never has to be skipped. An appearance you cannot afford is progress you have thrown away. Where Battle Fragments drop is a farming question of its own — treat any specific drop source you read as patch-sensitive and confirm in-game.
</Callout>

## Earthquake and the Spike-Ball Bounce

Earthquake is the fight's signature mechanic and its only real "technical" scoring point. Sung Lee leaps and slams down, and the slam deals damage where he lands. The interesting part is what it leaves behind.

If you fail to clear the impact, a **spiked ball** remains on the ground. You can step onto it to send it bouncing back at Sung Lee — a rare chance to convert the boss's own mechanic into damage. But the bounce happens **exactly once**: after that single reflection the ball bursts and scatters smaller spike balls across the floor.

<Callout type="warn" title="The bounce is a one-shot, not a loop">
This is the trap that separates players who have read the fight from players who have not. You do not get to keep kicking the spike ball. Reflect it once for damage, then immediately clear the small spike balls it scatters — those are new ground hazards, and they land in the exact spot you were just rewarded for standing in.
</Callout>

The lesson is a general one for the fight: the safe answer and the greedy answer are often the same tile for one second, and then they are opposites.

## Summon Weapon and Rain of Ruin

The other two abilities keep the pressure steady between Earthquakes:

- **Summon Weapon.** Sung Lee hurls weapons at you. This is the fight's ranged pressure — it forces you to keep moving even when the ground looks clear, and it punishes players who settle into a safe-looking pocket and stop reading the boss.
- **Rain of Ruin.** Thorns fall from above and blanket the ground, creating overlapping damage zones. Overlap is the whole danger: one thorn is a step, two stacked is a lethal patch. When Rain of Ruin is active, the arena effectively shrinks, and the ability to find the shrinking safe lane is the skill being tested.

The two together are why Sung Lee feels relentless. Summon Weapon denies you patience; Rain of Ruin denies you floor space.

## How to Score the Exam

If you can clear the trio's first two fights but stall here, the problem is almost always range discipline:

1. **Pick a fighting distance and stick to it.** Sung Lee punishes both melee greed and passive retreat. Ranged builds should not drift into his slam radius; melee builds should not stand in the ground zones. Know your class's safe band and treat it as a rule.
2. **Clear the impact, not the boss.** When Earthquake resolves, your first job is the spike ball and its debris — not re-engaging. Damage windows come after the ground is clean.
3. **Rebuild defensive cushion.** Because the fight is a long chain of small hits, effective health matters more than one big cooldown. The defensive spine in the [Incarnation Tree](/guides/hero-siege-incarnation-tree-guide/) is the usual fix for players who keep dying at 70%.
4. **Practice on the other two first.** Community consensus puts Sung Lee last in difficulty within the trio. If he feels unreasonable, [Reaper](/bosses/hero-siege-reaper-boss/) is the better preparation — the [Uber bosses overview](/bosses/hero-siege-uber-bosses-guide/) maps the whole ladder.

## War God Sung Lee FAQ

### Is Sung Lee worth farming once you can clear him?

Yes. He is one of the classic trio, so he is a consistent high-tier loot source and part of the standard weekly rotation. His drops feed the builds that later take on the modern and Season 10 Ubers.

### Which classes handle Sung Lee best?

Classes that can deal damage while moving have a structural advantage, because the fight constantly demands repositioning. Classes built around standing still and channeling have to work harder for the same result. The [class tier list](/guides/hero-siege-class-tier-list/) breaks down how each archetype tends to fare.

### Can I reflect the spike ball more than once?

No. The reflection is a single bounce — after the spike ball returns to Sung Lee it bursts and scatters smaller spike balls. Anyone describing a repeatable loop is describing a different, older behavior or misremembering; verify in-game.

### Why do I keep dying during Earthquake even when I dodge the slam?

Because the slam is only the first half. The spike ball and its scattered debris are the second half, and they arrive right where you were rewarded for standing. Plan your exit before you commit to the reflection.
