## What Does Inferno Change About Acts You Already Know?

Inferno is not new content — it is the same nine acts asking whether your build was ever actually good. Every enemy in the campaign comes back retuned at the top of the [four-tier difficulty ladder](/guides/hero-siege-difficulty-guide/): harder hits, bigger health pools, and boss fights that stop forgiving the mistakes lower tiers excuse. That is why the community treats a full Inferno clear as the campaign's real completion line rather than just another difficulty.

Two structural notes before the route. First, the act order does not move: Inferno runs the same fixed sequence, and each tier of the ladder had to be finished to unlock it. Second, Inferno is also where the within-tier difficulty slider earns its name — community documentation frames it as trading harder enemies for more reward and experience. Verify the current slider behavior in-game; the exact trade has moved with patches.

## What Should Be True Before You Start the Route?

Preparation for Inferno is mostly honest accounting. The four checks that matter:

1. **The build is finished, not promising.** Talents, skills and gear forming one coherent plan is the entry fee — the Act 3 lesson from the [Acts 1-3 walkthrough](/guides/hero-siege-act-walkthrough-1-3/) applied to the whole game. If the build needs "one more thing," the [starter builds page](/guides/hero-siege-league-starter-builds/) is the faster fix.
2. **Hell funded the gear.** The ladder is designed so each tier's farming pays for the next tier's upgrades; a route attempted on campaign leftovers stalls early. Gaps are closed fastest at the [market](/guides/hero-siege-market-guide/).
3. **Consumables are stocked.** Deaths on Inferno cost momentum, and cheap consumables buy clean retries.
4. **The route is known.** Nine acts is a long session; knowing where each act's boss waits keeps the run moving. The three walkthrough pages — [Acts 1-3](/guides/hero-siege-act-walkthrough-1-3/), [Acts 4-6](/guides/hero-siege-act-walkthrough-4-6/) and [Acts 7-9](/guides/hero-siege-act-walkthrough-7-9/) — cover each third zone by zone.

## Which Order Do the Nine Acts Follow on Inferno?

The same order as every difficulty — no skips, no reordering:

1. **Act 1 — Forests of Tarethiel**, boss: Gurag
2. **Act 2 — The Glacial Tundra**, boss: Grim Reaper
3. **Act 3 — Deserts of Mos'Arathim**, boss: Anubis
4. **Act 4 — Highland Caves**, boss: Damien
5. **Act 5 — Plains of Karponia**, boss: The Karp King
6. **Act 6 — Holy Grounds**, boss: Satan
7. **Act 7 — The Rift**, boss: Mevius
8. **Act 8 — Gates of Valhalla**, boss: Odin
9. **Act 9 — Abyssal Realm**, boss: Cthulhu

The finale keeps its double role on Inferno: Cthulhu is both the campaign's last fight and the gate fights behind the ladder's unlocks, which the [Cthulhu page](/bosses/hero-siege-cthulhu-boss/) documents — including the repeat-kill chain that made him the game's most-required boss.

## How Fast Can All Nine Inferno Acts Go?

The community's working benchmark for "Inferno-ready" is a full nine-act route inside a single session once the build comes together. That is a pace claim, not a rule — no official leaderboard exists, and class, deaths and retuning swings make specific times unreliable. What the benchmark captures is real: a finished build does not just survive Inferno, it moves through the acts at campaign speed.

## What Opens After the Ninth Inferno Boss?

The route's end is the endgame's front door. With the full campaign cleared at the top tier, the two tracks that outscale it take over: [wormhole pushing](/guides/hero-siege-wormhole-guide/) as the scaling loop where builds get stress-tested, and the [Uber roster](/bosses/hero-siege-uber-bosses-guide/) as the boss tier above the campaign in both difficulty and reward. The honest framing: the nine-act exam certifies you for the content that is actually hard.

## Inferno Route FAQ

**Should I farm Hell before starting the Inferno route?**
Usually, yes — briefly. The ladder hands you the ramp: Hell's farming pays for the gear the Inferno route assumes. Skipping that step does not make the route impossible, just slower and angrier.

**Where do Inferno runs usually stall?**
Wherever the build's weakest check was hiding. Lower tiers let a lopsided build coast until the boss that exposes it; on Inferno that is commonly the late acts, with the finale as the run's last gate — but the stall point follows the build, not the map.

**Does the Inferno route change per season?**
The route's shape holds — nine acts in fixed order — but tuning, new acts and reworks move around it, as Season 10's Act 9 and Nightmare rework proved. Check the [season guide](/guides/hero-siege-season-10-guide/) before planning a run around advice from an earlier season.
