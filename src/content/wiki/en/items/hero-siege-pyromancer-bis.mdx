## Why a Fire Caster's Shopping List Is Upside Down

Start with the counterpoint. The [Viking's BiS map](/items/hero-siege-viking-bis/) opens with one line: the weapon is the whole build, because spin damage scales straight off it. A [Pyromancer](/guides/hero-siege-pyromancer-build/) is the mirror image. Your damage is produced by the skills, amplified by fire and skill modifiers, so the weapon's white damage is close to irrelevant. Fill the modifier band first and the weapon last, and the whole slot map falls into place.

The order below follows from the caster's mechanics and holds across 7.0.x patches. Individual best items change often; confirm any specific piece against your own rolls before you commit gold to it.

## Slot One Is the Modifier Band, Not the Weapon

Everything above the weapon's damage line is what a fire caster actually buys:

1. **Skill-level and fire-damage modifiers — the engine.** These multiply the spells themselves, which is where the damage lives. They climb the rarity ladder first, from [Satanic](/items/hero-siege-satanic-items/) placeholders up toward peak tier.
2. **Cast speed — the second engine.** Faster casting is more damage per second and less exposure per cast. For a class that survives on distance, tempo is also a defensive stat.
3. **Area and duration effects — the multiplier band.** Extending how long fire lingers and how wide it spreads compounds across every pack.

## The Weapon Slot Is a Carrier

The weapon still earns a slot — just for what it carries, not for what it hits with. A fire caster wants a weapon that holds cast speed, skill modifiers or fire bonus, treating the physical damage roll as background noise. This is the single biggest mental switch coming from a melee class: you are shopping for the affixes bolted onto the weapon, not for the weapon's own number.

## The Survival Bill Your Own AoE Writes

Here is the part that separates a working Pyromancer from a stalled one. The build's best clear comes from casting *into* packs, which drags a thin health pool closer to danger than the class's reputation suggests. The slot map has to pay that bill:

- **A mitigation floor, not a tank build.** Enough life and damage reduction to survive a mistake, so a single overlap does not end the run.
- **Mobility as a defensive affix.** Movement pieces are not luxury — they are how a caster re-buys distance when a pack closes.
- **Recovery on kill or on cast.** Community-documented caster setups lean on sustain that triggers from the loop itself, keeping you topped up between packs.

The point is not to out-tank anyone. It is to stop the class's own aggression from becoming the thing that kills it.

## Filling the Band: Drops, Market, Codex

- **Drops** push the modifier band forward on the standard endgame loop — [wormholes](/guides/hero-siege-wormhole-guide/) and [Uber](/bosses/hero-siege-uber-bosses-guide/) rotations generate the tier pressure that upgrades each slot on schedule.
- **The [market](/guides/hero-siege-market-guide/)** is where a stalled slot becomes a solved one: buying the fire-modifier piece that unblocks endgame entry is a [gold-income](/guides/hero-siege-gold-farming-guide/) problem, not a luck problem.
- **The [Codex](/guides/hero-siege-codex-guide/)** is the finishing tool for a specific modifier roll, converting farmed material into attempts instead of waiting on RNG.

## When a Pyromancer Slot Is Done

A fire caster's slots hit a different finish line than a melee class's. A slot is done when the next upgrade would change your **rotation timing** rather than your hit numbers — when more cast speed stops shortening the gap between packs, or when the piece you would replace it with would not change how the loop feels. If an upgrade only nudges the damage line, it is a want, not a slot that is actually unfinished.

That definition matters because the Pyromancer's biggest gains are structural. A caster who chases raw damage past the point where cast speed and modifiers already carry the loop is buying numbers the build was never short on.

## Pyromancer BiS FAQ

### Should I buy a two-handed weapon for the higher damage roll?

Only if its affixes serve the caster. On a Pyromancer the weapon's own damage roll is background — a two-hander is worth taking when it carries more cast speed or fire modifiers, and worth skipping when it carries a big physical number you will never use.

### How much defense is enough?

Enough to survive one mistake per arena. The caster's range is its real armor; the defensive slots exist to convert a bad overlap into a scare instead of a death, not to let you stand in packs.

### Does the modifier band apply to the burst skill too?

Yes — skill and fire modifiers feed every button in the kit, which is exactly why they lead the slot map. The burst slot and the screen-clear both scale from the same band, so one good modifier piece upgrades the whole rotation at once.
