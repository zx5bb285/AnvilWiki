## What a Rune Word Actually Is

A rune word is a recipe that runs in your inventory. You take a **common** base item that has sockets, drop runes into those sockets in a **specific order**, and if everything matches the recipe, the item stops being an ordinary base and becomes a rune word with a fixed, designed effect.

Each rune carries its own effect on its own. The ordered combination is what produces the extra result — which is why a rune word is more precise than a random drop. Where hunting [uniques](/items/hero-siege-uniques-overview/) is a lottery, a rune word is a shopping list: decide what you want, then collect the parts.

Not every recipe makes armor. Community-documented potion-type rune words exist too (Ghostly Potion and Prismatic Potion are the examples most often cited) — so "rune word" means a crafted result, not strictly a wearable. Treat individual names as examples to confirm in-game.

<Callout type="info" title="System rules, not a specific recipe list">
The mechanics on this page are the stable scaffolding — they hold across seasons and patches. Exact recipes and best-in-slot rune words move season to season, so verify the specific one you are building before you spend rare runes.
</Callout>

## The Four Gates a Recipe Must Pass

Almost every failed craft is one of these four gates. Read them in order:

1. **Quality gate — the base must be common.** The item name shows gray. Magic, rare, myth and Satanic-quality bases are locked out entirely. A great-looking magic weapon can never become a rune word.
2. **Socket gate — the base must have sockets, and only certain slots can hold them.** Weapons, shields, helmets, armor and boots are the pieces that can be socketed. No sockets, no recipe.
3. **Match gate — socket count and item type must line up exactly.** A two-rune recipe needs a two-socket base of the right type. Put it in a three-socket piece and you get a common item with runes in it — nothing transforms.
4. **Order gate — the runes go in the exact sequence the recipe names.** The same runes in a different order do not produce the rune word.

<Callout type="warn" title="The expensive mistake">
Gate three is where people burn rare runes. The rule of thumb: if the result did not visibly transform, the first thing to check is whether the socket count and base type matched — not whether the runes were rare enough.
</Callout>

## Runeword Bases: Where the Gray Items Come From

Because the base has to be plain and socketed, the [market](/guides/hero-siege-market-guide/) and low-stakes farming are your base suppliers, not boss loot.

- **Sold and dropped commons.** Normal play hands you socketed common pieces constantly; for a low-tier recipe, your inventory is already full of candidates.
- **High item-level bases.** Most rune words require a base at item level 100 or above — the community calls a qualifying piece a "Runeword Base." Those come from higher-[difficulty](/guides/hero-siege-difficulty-guide/) content, and buying one directly is often cheaper than farming a specific socket count.
- **The Act 1 cube.** The cube also participates in the rune economy itself (see below), and community writeups list it among the routes to useful base and rune material.

## The Rune Economy's Counter-Intuitive Price Map

If you only learn one pricing fact, learn this: **rune number does not track rune value.**

- **Ber and Zed usually sit at the top.** They appear in so many high-end recipes that demand keeps them expensive regardless of drop volume.
- **JAH and QI are top-tier runes that can still be cheap.** Their demand is narrower, so the market prices them below their rarity suggests. Buying "the highest number" is a good way to overpay.
- **HEL is in constant demand for a non-crafting reason** — it is consumed by socket punching, so it trades above its tier.
- **The cube and the decomposer move value in both directions.** Act 1's cube combines three lower runes into one higher rune; the decomposer takes a higher rune back down into lower ones. That two-way valve is what lets you shop for value instead of praying for a specific drop. Random rune drops are gated by area and monster level, and some also come from specific monsters, quests or cube work — all community-documented, so confirm sources in-game.

## Punching Sockets: Two Hel and One Perfect Gem

If your base has no sockets, the community-documented punching recipe is **2× Hel Rune + 1× Perfect Gem**. Two details matter:

1. It works **once per item** — you cannot punch a piece twice, so think about the base before you commit.
2. It eats HEL, which is exactly why HEL trades above its tier. When you plan a socket-punch, price the HEL into the project.

## Upgrade Rules: What Tokens Can and Cannot Touch

Rune words are not frozen at creation. The rules worth knowing before you feed a token into one:

- **Upgrade Tokens or Rubies** can push a rune word up to a maximum of level 10.
- **Ability Tokens** apply to rune word weapons only.
- **Re-roll Tokens and Satanic Dices do not work on rune words** — do not plan a reroll project around one.

## The Errors That Quietly Waste Runes

- **Socketing into a magic or rare base.** It looks identical to a working craft until nothing transforms.
- **Mismatching socket count.** The extra socket is not "room to grow" — it is a failed recipe.
- **Rushing the order.** Sequence is part of the recipe; it is not a suggestion.
- **Underpricing HEL.** Punching sockets looks cheap until you price the HEL demand it shares with every other builder.
- **Ignoring the [Codex](/guides/hero-siege-codex-guide/) route.** Codex-related rune word paths exist alongside the raw recipes — worth checking before you commit a base to a random experiment.

## Rune Words FAQ

### Where do rune word bases actually come from?

Practically anywhere that drops socketed commons — normal play, higher-difficulty farming, and the [market](/guides/hero-siege-market-guide/). For a specific socket count on a high item-level base, buying it is usually faster than farming it. Verify the base's level and socket count in-game before you socket anything.

### Does socketing order really matter?

Yes. Order is part of the recipe, not flavor text. The same runes in the wrong sequence do not produce the rune word — which is why it is worth reading the recipe twice before you touch the base.

### Is a rune word better than a unique?

They solve different problems. A rune word is predictable — you choose the result, then collect the parts. A unique is a lottery drop that may be stronger in a given slot. The honest answer is that a rune word is the *reliable* route, not automatically the *strongest* one, and which you want depends on whether you value certainty or ceiling.
