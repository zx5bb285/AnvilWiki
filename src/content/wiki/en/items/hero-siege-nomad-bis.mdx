## The Hybrid Tax, Paid in Slots

A single-damage class has a simple bookkeeping problem: every affix that serves its damage type is progress. A [Nomad](/guides/hero-siege-nomad-build/) runs two damage routes — a physical one and a magic one — and that flexibility has a price. The split is not cosmetic: the class's damage skills scale off two different attributes, so a Strength-leaning kit and an Energy-leaning kit are genuinely different builds sharing one character. Each slot has a limited budget of affix lines, and a hybrid has to decide how many of those lines go to each route. Spend evenly and both halves stay mediocre. This page is about the tax and how to stop paying it.

<Callout type="info" title="The patch caveat">
Which route is stronger shifts with balance patches, and community docs are not unanimous. Treat the guidance here as a decision framework — it stays correct even when the raw numbers move. Verify specific items in-game.
</Callout>

## Choose a Primary Before You Shop

The Nomad starts the season broad. Endgame gear has to narrow it. Before you spend anything serious, name a primary route — and let the loot name it for you:

- **If your best drops skew physical** (weapon damage, physical bonus, attack speed), physical is your primary. Keep it.
- **If they skew magic** (spell and magic-damage affixes), make magic primary.
- **If neither has shown up yet**, gear the movement and survival core and stay undecided — but decide before you commit peak-tier pieces, because a peak piece for the wrong route is the most expensive mistake this class makes.

## The Cut List: What Dies When a Route Dies

Committing means subtraction. When you cut a route, these are the things that go with it:

1. **Damage-type affixes for the dropped route.** This is the obvious one — every physical roll on a magic Nomad is a wasted line.
2. **Route-specific skill investment.** Talents and [Ether Tree](/guides/hero-siege-ether-tree-guide/) branches that only feed the cut route are budget you get back for the survivor.
3. **Hybrid-driven item picks.** Pieces you bought only because they helped *both* halves stop being worth their slot once there is only one half — replace them with a stronger single-route piece at the same tier.

The sacrifice feels like a downgrade because your sheet loses a damage type. It is not: a focused board clears faster than a balanced one at the same gear level, and it reaches the next [rarity tier](/items/hero-siege-uniques-overview/) sooner.

## Spending the Last Affix Lines

Once a primary exists, the end of the board is about the spare lines — the affixes after your core stats are covered. A hybrid's instinct is to spend them on the secondary route "for coverage." That is the trap. Spend them on the primary's ceiling instead:

- **More of the primary's multiplier** beats a token amount of the secondary's.
- **Movement stays worth it on both routes** — the Nomad is still a mobile class, and mobility is the one budget line that never gets cut.
- **Survival last.** The class's speed is its armor; a thin defensive band is enough once positioning is habit.

## Filling a Focused Board

A committed Nomad fills its slots like any endgame class, with a shorter shopping list:

- **Drops** push the primary route's slots forward on the usual [wormhole](/guides/hero-siege-wormhole-guide/) and endgame loop.
- **The [market](/guides/hero-siege-market-guide/)** is where the cut pays off most — with one route to fund instead of two, the gold you would have split now buys a stronger single piece. That is a [gold-income](/guides/hero-siege-gold-farming-guide/) problem turned into an advantage.
- **Heroic-tier and up pieces** are where the commitment compounds: a [Heroic](/items/hero-siege-heroic-items/) item for one route outperforms two mid-tier items split across both.

## When a Nomad Slot Is Done

The finish line for this class is not power — it is **the absence of compromise**. A Nomad slot is done when every affix line on it is spent on the route you kept, with nothing left hedging the route you cut. If a piece is still half-serving the dropped route, it is not finished no matter how good its primary stats look.

That is why the Nomad's gearing feels slower at first and faster later. The class spends the early season deciding, then spends the rest of it banking on one answer — and a slot that has stopped being a compromise is the only kind worth peak-tier investment.

## Nomad BiS FAQ

### Can I gear both routes on a budget set?

For a while, yes — a budget hybrid set clears the campaign, which is partly why the class is forgiving early. The problem appears at endgame tier, where splitting a limited affix budget across two damage types caps both below where a single-route set would sit.

### Does the Nomad still want movement gear after committing?

Yes — movement never joins the cut list. It is the class's identity and its defense, and it pays out on either route. The cut is about damage-type affixes, not about the mobility core.

### What is the most common Nomad gearing mistake?

Spending rare affix lines "just in case" on the route you did not keep. Those lines are pure opportunity cost: they do nothing for your damage and they are exactly what a focused board would have used to reach the next tier.
